*How these notes were gathered:* youtube.com, playables.games, youtube-playables.fandom.com, play.google.com, apps.apple.com, bluestacks.com and reddit.com were all **unreachable** from this machine (proxy 403 / DNS failure). Facts below come from (a) search-engine summaries of those pages (they can garble a page — one contradicted itself about a rank — so single-summary facts are marked M or L), (b) two GitHub mirrors of YouTube's own Playables catalogue data, fetched raw, and (c) GitHub code search. **vidIQ**, the only route to YouTube's own data, ran out of credits after one call (resets 2026-10-24), so there are **no YouTube view counts, comment text or transcripts** here. Confidence tags: **H** = two independent sources or primary data; **M** = one decent source; **L** = one weak source, or a summary that may be garbled.

## Q1. Which draw-to-save / draw-a-line / physics-rescue puzzles are live on YouTube Playables now (Oct 2026), who makes them, and how big are they?

### Takeaway
The closest live rival is **Save The Pets** (SUN.STUDIO). It is a draw-a-line-to-protect-the-dog-from-bees game with **~38M plays, #24 of ~1,850 Playables (18 Oct 2026)**. The biggest draw-input puzzles are **Thief Puzzle** (~90–95M, #1 in Brain & Puzzle) and **DOP 4: Draw One Part** (~41–42M). There are at least three smaller "save the dog" clones (6.1M, 3.5M, and two added on 6 Oct 2026), so this niche on Playables is **proven and getting crowded right now**. I found no Playables listing for the genre's best-known originals, Save the Doge and Love Balls.

## Q3. Save The Pets and its closest rivals: mechanic, hazards, win condition, levels, cadence, difficulty, hints and skips, stars, cosmetics, daily, ads, art, session length

### Takeaway
Save The Pets is **Save the Doge's formula**:
- draw one line;
- a hive of bees (and later lava, water, spikes and bombs) attacks a pet;
- **survive 10 seconds** to win;
- **more stars for a shorter line**;
- an "unlimited" level stream on mobile, which reviewers say **starts repeating after ~100–200 levels**.

Its sibling rivals win on volume and a one-second-readable premise. On mobile they lose players to ad load. **On Playables, in-app purchases are not allowed** and ads must use YouTube's own ad calls. So the mobile ad-and-IAP model does not carry over directly, and how much these ports actually advertise is unknown.

## Q4. What do players say — what they love, and what makes them quit?

### Takeaway
Across all four mobile originals the number-one complaint is **ad load**:
- "an ad after every single level";
- "1.5 seconds to solve, 2 minutes of ads";
- **paid no-ads that stops working**.

Next come **content that repeats** (Save The Pets after ~100–200 levels), **progress walls you can only pass by watching an ad** (hints at ~level 43; Save the Doge's new puzzle mode), and **physics or solutions that feel unfair** — bees clipping through walls, unclear solutions, correct drawings rejected. What players praise: cute and fun, a relaxed pace, helpful hints, a difficulty ramp. Professional critics see Playables as a shelf of ad-game clones.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **What makes players quit, in order:**
  1. ads after every level;
  2. pay-to-progress gates;
  3. repetition, from about the hundredth level;
  4. failures that feel like the game's fault — clipping bees, a correct answer rejected, unclear solutions.
- Our design already targets 2–4: no level is sold, no two levels ask the same decision, the fairness gates, and the death cam. Item 1 is a choice we keep making well (see Q6).
- "A line from one end to the other solves it" is Save the Doge's version of our A14 "decoration" finding. Players notice when a level does not need its hazard.
- Clipping bees in Save the Doge mean a player once drew a correct shield and lost anyway. Our bees tests and close-call line-of-sight rule are exactly the protection against that review.

## Q6. What is our game missing compared with them, and where are we ahead?

### Takeaway
**Missing:**
- volume — 42 levels against "hundreds" or "unlimited";
- a premise readable in one second around a cute victim on a clock;
- earned cosmetics and seasonal events;
- a visible progress meter;
- a steady content drip;
- the words "save" and "draw" in the name, for search.

**Ahead** on exactly what reviewers punish them for:
- no repeated levels;
- no ad-gated walls, and a skip granted even when no ad is available;
- a lighter ad load;
- physics checked for fairness, with a death cam that shows why you lost;
- a measured difficulty curve;
- a daily challenge;
- several elements beyond a bee swarm.

### Cited Findings
**What they have (rivals)**

**Our side, for comparison** (from this repo's CLAUDE.md, the project's own record; not external)
- Interstitial every **3rd** level, never before level 3, never on the ending.
- One rewarded ad, used only as an **opt-in skip** after 3–5 fails, granted anyway if no ad loads.
- A UTC **daily** at three-star ink.
- Stars for short lines.
- A **death cam** replaying the loss.
- Gates for solvability, fairness and hand-robustness.
- A difficulty curve fitted by equation.
- Elements: rocks, TNT, updrafts and downdrafts, balloons, brittle panes, levers, plates and gates, bees, a dog.
- The rule that **no two levels ask the same decision**.
- Planned in docs/CASE_STUDY.md but not built: a star shop for cosmetics, and weekly batches.

### Inferences
- **Copy, low cost and high fit:**
  1. a **level-1 that cannot be lost** and a one-line premise on the first frame ("Get Milo out — he's cooked in 10…"). A visible short clock could suit our hazard levels.
  2. an **earned** cosmetic shop paid in stars. Playables forbids IAP, so rivals cannot out-sell us there, and a seasonal skin (Halloween is 31 Oct) is the event pattern Save The Pets uses.
  3. a **chapter-progress meter** on the board (TapNation +15%, self-reported).
  4. a daily-reward streak around the existing daily.
  5. pet framing for the dog — "save the dog" is the genre's most searched fantasy.
- **Avoid:**
  1. ads per level — our every-third rule is already lighter than every rival reviewed;
  2. ad-gated progress;
  3. padding volume with near-repeats — reviewers notice at ~100;
  4. any physics that lets a correct shield fail, such as bee clipping. Keep the bees and death-cam tests strict.
- **Positioning:** on a shelf of interchangeable "save the dog" clones (Save The Pets, Doge Rescue, Save the Dog-Puzzle game, Love Doge), our edge is **variety of physics and honesty of failure**. The risk is the opposite: a stranger may not grasp a varied game in one glance the way they grasp "bees vs dog".
- **Discoverability:** the genre's titles all contain "Save" or "Draw". "BRO, YOU'RE COOKED." contains neither. The Playables description and any companion Shorts should carry "draw a line to save him".
- **Volume:** 42 levels is far below the rivals. Even Playables-native draw games like Draw Bridge (~21M plays) and Save The Pets come from large mobile level banks. The case study's answer — variants plus a weekly drip — still stands. Repetition at ~100 levels is the failure mode to stay ahead of.

### Gaps
- We do not know the Playables builds' level counts, ad cadence, hints or skins (see Q3), so the comparison of Playables versions against ours is partly against the **mobile** originals.
- No retention or session data for any rival on Playables. YouTube does not publish it, and TubeGames tracks only plays.
- Whether a 10-second survival clock would fit our route-to-exit levels is a design question for playtesting, not something research can answer.
