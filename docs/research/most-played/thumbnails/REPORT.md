# Thumbnails and store pages: what we learned, and what it means for BYC (9 Nov 2026)

Written by the lead after reading three helper reports (`agents/a1-publishers.md`, `agents/a2-tiles-measured.md`, `agents/a3-youtubers-descriptions.md`) and checking them: six of A2's hand-coded tiles re-read against the images (6 of 6 match); A1's Supersonic claims re-checked against the App Store seller field and Supersonic's own 2023 icon post (match); A3's YouTube Test & Compare and Save The Pets claims re-checked on the live pages (match). Tags: [MEASURED] = counted or seen this session; [REPORTED] = a source says so; [INFERRED] = reasoning; UNVERIFIED = could not confirm. Nothing here proves a cover earns plays: no click-through data exists outside the platforms.

## Why the cover and the page text matter (plain words)
A player meets BYC as a small picture in a grid of hundreds of games, 194 px wide on the YouTube hub or about 221 px wide on Playgama. They decide in about a second. Every play begins with that click, and the page text is what a hesitating visitor and a search engine read next. Two equally good games can get very different numbers of plays because one tile says clearly what the game is and the other does not. Two things keep this honest. First, a tile that over-promises is punished: YouTube's own A/B test for thumbnails picks the winner on watch time, not on clicks [MEASURED: support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714], and YouTube bans misleading thumbnails. Second, the one big publisher that publishes its testing (Supersonic) reports that showing the real mechanic beat a generic icon: +30% store conversion for Emoji Puzzle by replacing a laughing face with the actual emojis joined by a line, +15% for Tall Man Run by showing its real gates and character [REPORTED: Supersonic blog, 10 Jan 2023, first-party, selected wins, no sample sizes, app-store icons not these platforms]. So packaging is worth real effort, and it is tested, not guessed.

## What the evidence says (each tied to a helper table)
1. [MEASURED] Where BYC will be seen: Playgama cards are mostly landscape 16:9 images (448x252 served, boxes about 221x148 to 450x277), with some vertical 500x820 and square 218x218 tiles; the card image on the draft is the LANDSCAPE cover. The YouTube hub shows portrait 5:7 tiles (194x272). The landscape cover is the one most Playgama visitors will see; the square is the smallest (123 px box).
2. [MEASURED] Looks alone do not predict plays. Across 1,124 hub tiles the strongest rank correlation is edge density at -0.23 (busier tiles have slightly fewer plays), then contrast at -0.16; saturation is -0.10. Playgama's top and trending tiles are nearly identical to the rest. Confounds: publisher size, time on platform, distribution, personalised ordering. (A2)
3. [MEASURED] What most winning tiles DO share: one large subject; the game's own name as large lettering on about 65% of tiles (39 of 60 hub, 38 of 60 Playgama in A2's sample; 71 of 77 lettered tiles carry the game's name); a moment of action or change on 55-62% of tiles; a clean, bright, saturated background. The top hub tiles are calmer, not louder, than the tail. (A2, A1)
4. [MEASURED] The biggest publishers do not agree on lettering: Supersonic tiles carry the title 1 of 13, CrazyLabs 0 of 8, SayGames 12 of 13, Voodoo 12 of 13, Supercent 7 of 7. Both groups have games above 100m plays. (A1)
5. [MEASURED] The draw/rescue genre's convention on Playgama (18 coded tiles): a face on 17, a visible or implied threat on 12, 2D illustration on 12. The hub's high-ranking doodle tiles (Thief Puzzle, Save The Pets, DOP) sit on light backgrounds with large decorative titles and small characters. By my own eye on the contact sheets (not coded data): most Playgama puzzle tiles show a hand cursor performing the verb. (A2, lead)
6. [MEASURED] Where BYC's own covers stand: the 'pop' set has ordinary colour (52nd to 69th percentile) but is flatter than almost every tile (edge density at the 0th percentile of Playgama tiles, 2nd to 9th of hub tiles), has no lettering and its landscape version cuts Milo's body. The 'paper' set is the palest picture on either platform (79 to 83% pale pixels against a median of 3 to 5%). Neither should be a final cover. (A2)
7. [REPORTED] Supersonic validates games and art by testing: ad-video cost per install before building (2020 post), icon variants for conversion (2023 post); creative teams make several versions and keep the winner. (A1) [INFERRED] The lesson for BYC is to ship more than one cover over time and keep a dated log, not to find one perfect design.
8. [REPORTED/MEASURED] YouTubers: MrBeast's first-party statements are few (he sketches thumbnails before filming; the aim is to make people feel something); the detailed principles come second-hand from his thumbnail lead. Across 70 thumbnails of 7 creator sets: one large focal subject, one visible stake, 0 to 3 words, saturated clean backgrounds, and a pointer (ring or arrow) when the cause is small. In the exact 'save the dog from bees' genre the designed tiles show the GAME's character large with the swarm and a reaction. Published research is thin, about video not game tiles, and mostly vendor-funded. (A3)
9. [MEASURED] Playgama game pages: every one of 60 uses a template (About, How to Play, Controls, often Key Features and an FAQ), median about 320 words; 'How to Play' on 57 of 60; the page title and meta description are formulaic and generated, with no field in the cabinet. Playgama's own Wrap guide says content fields affect search ranking and conversion (written for Wrap sites, not the catalog page). The wiki sets no rule on description or cover wording. (A3)
10. [MEASURED] BYC has a near-twin: Save The Pets: Draw to Save (Bravestars on Playgama, rating 4.5 from 209 votes; a dog, bees, draw a line, stars for a shorter line; rank 26 on the hub at 39m plays). Both the covers and the text must say what BYC does differently: Milo, the world freezing a heartbeat before disaster, 45 levels in seven chapters, the daily. (A3, A1)
11. UNVERIFIED: whether Playgama or YouTube Playables offers any cover A/B facility (neither helper found one); whether the Playgama card shows the title under the image (if it does, a title on the art is redundant there); whether a title wordmark counts as a 'logo' under YouTube's design rule.

## What does NOT transfer (and is not allowed)
A person's face, name or branding (MrBeast's or anyone's), '$' amounts, referee shirts, franchise characters or logos, fake 'PLAY NOW' buttons, instruction sentences baked into the art. Playgama 4.2.3 to 4.2.5 and YouTube's no-logo and no-misleading rules. The Manus case study the owner supplied is a video-structure study; its section 6 says the same.

## Limits
Hand coding by one rater (me and helpers), non-random samples; Playgama sample is landscape only (no vertical or square Playgama tiles were coded); publisher attribution mostly from one third-party tracker (35 of 40 single-source); no CTR, retention or impression data for any tile; vendor studies; one crawl on 6 Nov and one on 9 Nov.
