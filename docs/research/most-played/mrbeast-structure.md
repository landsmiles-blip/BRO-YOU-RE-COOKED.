## 3. Devices found

Each device has the evidence and a grade. "Click" devices are marked [C]; "session" devices [S].

1. **D1 Premise-in-the-first-sentence cold open [C/S].** Every long video states the stake and a named obstacle in 0:00-0:16 (Circle, Ronaldo, Haaland, Squid). MEASURED.
2. **D2 First consequence within 20-50 s.** Squid gunshot about 0:17; Circle first elimination about 0:49; Haaland first match result by 2:28 but first "scoring" within 1 min. MEASURED.
3. **D3 Module blocks of about 2-6 minutes**, each with its own stake and mini-result (Ronaldo 6 modules, Haaland 7). MEASURED; my reading of the boundaries.
4. **D4 Handicap rule that makes the underdog winnable** (smaller balloons, head start priced in dollars, half fence, 50x hole, bigger target). MEASURED. This is what makes each module an uncertain bet.
5. **D5 Stakes stepped by rank and by money** (Ronaldo: $100k for early modules, $1M at the end; Circle: 100 players to 10 to 5 to 2). MEASURED.
6. **D6 Forward teasers and called-back payoffs** ("later in this video", "just wait until Ronaldo", then pay off: IShowSpeed raced Lyles at 16:06-18:20). MEASURED.
7. **D7 Re-hook by new rule or format change every few minutes** (Circle: towers to hot potato to balance beam to player-picked game to paintball; "half of you go home" at 8:19). MEASURED. Reporting says the internal document calls for a "re-engagement" at about 3 and 6 minutes (REPORTED, secondhand, snippet only).
8. **D8 Countdown on screen / timer you cannot see** (24-hour tower timer, hidden 60-minute timer, 1-minute choice timer, "Seven seconds. Lock in!" in Haaland). MEASURED.
9. **D9 Player choice moments** (contestants choose the game; choose endurance or game, written on a sign). MEASURED.
10. **D10 Cast reactions and crowd as emotion carriers** (cheering schoolmates, a father walked in at about 35:12, chants). MEASURED.
11. **D11 Sponsor turned into a mini-beat in the middle** (Yahoo at 12:47, Shopify at about 16:21 and 13:49) with an in-video wink; in 2018 it was a separate chapter-length "game spot" at 1:53 for about 98 s. MEASURED. Whether the newer approach retains better: REPORTED that a retention chart "shows less dramatic dip" with integrated deals (snippet only).
12. **D12 Payoff placement: climax around 82-98% of runtime, with smaller payoffs at each module end.** Circle winner at 37:55 of 38:38; Ronaldo at 21:52 of 22:45; Haaland LeBron 22:01 of 22:28. MEASURED. The Most replayed graph on Circle has its biggest non-start peak at the winner. MEASURED (approx.).

## 4. Translation to the first 60 seconds of a draw-puzzle Playable

Context: the "click" is a tile tap on the Playables shelf (another agent's scope). Here the "hook" is first-frame to first-line. Video watching is passive; a puzzle session is active, which changes several of these.

| Device | Game equivalent in the first 60 s of BRO, YOU'RE COOKED. | Transfers? | Why / limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Premise in first sentence | First frame IS the level: a bro on a ledge over lava/anvil with the line "SAVE HIM". No title screen, no tutorial pages. First stroke drawn in under 5 s. | Yes | Costs nothing; every cold open above does it. |
| D2 First consequence in 20-50 s | A wrong or no-line first try triggers a visible "COOKED" fail by about 10-20 s; level 1 should be solvable with any sensible line so first win lands by 20 s. | Yes | The video equivalent is the first elimination. Fail must be fast and funny, with one-tap retry. |
| D3 Modules of 2-6 min | 3-4 micro-levels of 10-20 s each in the first 60 s. | Partly | Scale is 10x smaller; keep the "module with its own mini-result" idea, not the minutes. |
| D4 Handicap that makes underdog winnable | Early levels give a generous ink budget or a forgiving hazard, with a "big hole" visual cue; shrink the help later. | Yes | Keeps the "I might win this" feeling. |
| D5 Stakes stepped | Level 1: bro falls to a pillow. Level 3: three bros / a cop chase. Visible stakes jump by level 3 (by about 45 s). | Partly | No money stake; use number of bros or height. |
| D6 Forward teasers | Silhouette of the next hazard on the win screen ("next: SHARKS"); a locked boss tile visible from level 1. | Partly | Teaser must be visible without extra taps; not proven to raise retention in games. |
| D7 Re-hook by new rule every few minutes | A new mechanic or object at level 3 or 4 (about 40-60 s): water, a conveyor, a second bro. | Yes | Matches D7 timing scaled to puzzle time. |
| D8 Countdown | Optional timer pressure on a single level (a rising flood, a falling anvil); do not make it the default. | Partly | Timers help tension but punish thinking in a puzzle; the player controls pace here, unlike a viewer. |
| D9 Player choice | Choose between two tools (pencil or thick marker) or two paths once; or choose which bro to save. | Partly | Choice must change outcome or it is noise. |
| D10 Reaction cutaways | Bro reaction emotes after each fail/win; sound sting. | Yes | Cheap emotion carrier. |
| D11 Sponsor as a beat | Any ad after a win or on retry, never mid-draw; an incentivised retry/hint as a "beat". | Partly | Policy on ads in Playables is outside this research; placement idea only. |
| D12 Payoff placement | A tiny payoff every level plus a bigger one at level 4 or 5. | Yes | Direct parallel to module payoffs and the climax near the end. |
| D13 Hand-off | Win screen auto-advances to the next level on one tap; no menu in between. | Yes | Equivalent of "next module"; there is no "next video" in a Playable except the shelf. |
| D14 Short structure | The 60-s game = stake, choice, twist, result, repeat. | Yes | Literal parallel of the 48-s Short. |
| D15 Fast cuts | None; keep the player's own pace. | No | The cut cadence is an edit trick for passive viewing. MrBeast himself said he slowed down in 2024 (REPORTED). |
| D16 Process (retention curve, hook testing) | Funnel per level: drop-off graph of level 1-5, first-30-s analysis; iterate the first 20 s most. | Yes | Method transfers even if his thresholds (e.g., minute 1/3/6) do not. |

What does not transfer: celebrity cast and names in a title, large cash stakes, ad-integrated storytelling, a 20-40 minute session driven by a narrated payoff, and any "click" mechanism that depends on thumbnail faces.

## 5. Counter-evidence and surprises

- **Format break, still huge:** The Short "Subscribe for an iPhone" (15 s, 1.23B views) and "Giving iPhones Instead Of Candy" (40 s, 1.95B) have almost no setup (vidIQ, MEASURED). Short and simple can beat module-heavy.
- **Long is not penalized:** "Eat Everything In A Grocery Store, Win $1,000,000" (58 min, 324M views, breakout 2.6, vidIQ) and "Lose 150 Lbs Together" (43 min, 179M) show that long videos can have the highest relative views (MEASURED). So "short sessions" is not a rule for him.
- **Off-pattern and relatively weaker:** "Click This Video To Feed 1 Person" (10:29, breakout 0.38) and "Hi Me In 10 Years" (3:14, breakout 0.38) have far lower relative views than his challenge videos, yet the highest like-to-view rates (about 5.3% and 5.1%; my arithmetic from vidIQ data). Not a retention measure.
- **2018 pattern differs:** A 98-second sponsor spot at 1:53 in a 31.7M-view video (MEASURED chapters) violates the "no dead second" rule as it is described today, so the rule is era-specific or the video's draw was the premise alone. INFERRED.
- **Pacing reversal:** MrBeast posted in March 2024 that he had slowed videos down and urged others to (REPORTED, Gigazine). Yet the 2025 and 2026 transcripts here still show rapid-fire reactive speech (INFERRED from line density).
- **Most replayed peaks are about the climax, not the hook:** On Circle and Ronaldo the biggest non-start peak is the climax. This suggests viewers rewatch payoffs; it does not show who dropped out in between.
